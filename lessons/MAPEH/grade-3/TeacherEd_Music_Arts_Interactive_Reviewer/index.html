Your Turn!

I • LEARN • 10 min

🎵 Three Key Ideas

Pitch is the highness or lowness of sound. Rhythm concerns the value/duration of notes and rests—the length of sound or silence. When pitch and rhythm are combined, they create melody.

Remember: Pitch tells us “how high or low.” Rhythm tells us “how long.” Melody is the tune created when these musical elements work together.
L • EXPLORE • 10 min

🔎 Spot the Difference

Imagine four notes with the same duration but different heights. The change in height is a change in pitch. Now imagine notes at the same height but with different durations. That difference belongs to rhythm.

🐸 Notes Like Hopping Frogs

📈 Four Movement Ideas

Up + step-wiseNotes rise through neighboring line/space positions.
Down + step-wiseNotes fall through neighboring positions.
Up + skip-wiseNotes rise with a skip between positions.
Down + skip-wiseNotes fall with a skip.
RepeatingNotes stay on the same line or space.

DAY 3 • ARTS

What Is Space in Art?

Date: __________ • Estimated time: 50 minutes

Daily target: Define space as an element of art and explain how drawings can create an illusion of depth on a 2-D surface.
MOTIVATION • 5 min

🏝️ Near, Far, Very Far

Look at a landscape. A large palm tree appears near, while smaller trees and mountains appear farther away. Ask: “How did the artist make your eyes feel distance?”

Your Turn!

Which is a basic element of art discussed in this lesson?

I • LEARN • 10 min

🖼️ Definition

Space refers to the distance or area between, around, above, below, or within objects. It is one of the basic elements of art.

Artists represent a three-dimensional world on a two-dimensional surface. Techniques can create an illusion of depth, making a drawing look more realistic.

Key question: “Does the picture make an object look close, far, or very far?”
L • EXPLORE • 10 min

🔎 Look for Depth

Study three visual clues: size, overlapping, and placement. Before checking the answer, explain what your eyes notice.

Size differenceSimilar objects that appear farther away are represented smaller.
OverlappingAn object in front can partially cover another object, making the covered one appear farther back.
PlacementObjects positioned higher on the picture plane generally appear farther away; lower objects generally appear closer.

DAY 4 • ARTS + INTEGRATION

Creating Depth: Size, Overlap & Placement

Date: __________ • Estimated time: 50 minutes

Daily target: Select and apply appropriate space/depth techniques, then connect the idea of “shape/direction” in visual art and music.
MOTIVATION • 5 min

👀 Which Looks Closer?

Imagine two similar houses. One is large at the bottom of the picture; the other is smaller and higher. Which appears closer?

I • LEARN • 10 min

📐 Three Techniques

1. Size Difference: farther similar objects look smaller.

2. Overlapping: a closer object can partially cover a farther object.

3. Placement of Objects: objects higher in the picture plane generally appear farther away, while lower objects generally appear closer.

WEEKLY SUMMARY

🌟 What We Learned This Week

  • Melody: the horizontal arrangement of notes in the musical staff; the tune that you sing.
  • Melodic direction: the step or skip movement between two notes.
  • Melodic contour: the overall shape of a melody.
  • Space: distance/area between, around, above, below, or within objects.
  • Depth techniques: size difference, overlapping, placement of objects.

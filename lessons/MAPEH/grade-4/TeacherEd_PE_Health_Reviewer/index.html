PHYSICAL EDUCATION

Kickball: striking & fielding

Kickball is a striking-and-fielding game. The offensive team kicks a ball and tries to score; the defensive team fields the ball and tries to get runners out.

Skills and memory cues
  • Target / striking drills: practise eye–foot coordination by kicking toward a safe target.
  • Running drills: practise speed, changing direction, and running around cones safely.
  • Throwing and catching: use controlled throws and watch the ball into your hands.
  • Teamwork: communicate, take turns, and pass accurately.

Remember: KICK → RUN → FIELD → THROW / CATCH.

HEALTH

Pathogens & communicable disease

A pathogen is a disease-causing agent. A communicable disease can spread directly or indirectly from one person or source to another.

Four pathogen groups
  • Bacteria: single-celled organisms; some cause diseases.
  • Viruses: need living cells to multiply.
  • Fungi: include yeasts and molds; some cause infections such as ringworm.
  • Parasites: live on or in a host and obtain nutrients from it.

Four groups: Bacteria · Viruses · Fungi · Parasites.

Examples in the textbook include tuberculosis and boils (bacterial), flu and measles (viral), athlete’s foot and ringworm (fungal), and pinworm or hookworm infections (parasitic).

PREVENTION

How pathogens spread

Possible routes include air droplets, contaminated food or water, dirty hands, wounds, bodily fluids, bites, soil, and contaminated shared objects. The route depends on the pathogen.

Practical prevention
  • Wash hands with soap and safe water.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes; improve ventilation when possible.
  • Eat properly handled food and drink safe water.
  • Clean and cover wounds; avoid sharing personal items such as combs.
  • Wear footwear where soil may be contaminated and follow local health guidance.

Clean hands, safe food/water, cover coughs, protect wounds, don’t share personal items.

THINK DEEPER

Correct the misconception

Not every illness is communicable. Toothache and asthma do not spread from person to person like a common cold can.

Use evidence, not guesses
  • A person having symptoms does not prove they infected someone.
  • Different pathogens spread in different ways.
  • Prevention should match the route of transmission.
  • For health concerns, consult a qualified health professional rather than self-diagnosing.

Ask: What causes it? How does it spread? What prevention matches that route?

📖 READ

Dust Under the Rug

There was once a mother who had two little daughters. Her husband was dead and she was very poor. She worked faithfully so that they might be well-fed and clothed. She was a skilled worker, but she worked away from home. Her two little girls were good and helpful and kept their house neat and bright.

One of the little girls was lame and could not run about the house, so she sat still on her chair and sewed. Minnie, the older one, washed the dishes, swept the floor, and made the home beautiful.

Their home was on the edge of a great forest. After their tasks were finished, the little girls would sit by the window. They watched the tall trees bend in the wind as though the trees were real persons nodding, bending, and bowing to each other. In spring there were birds, in summer wild flowers, in autumn bright leaves, and in winter great drifts of white snow. The whole year was a round of delight to the two happy children.

One day the mother came home sick, and this made the children very sad. It was winter, and there were many things to buy. Minnie and her sister sat by the fire and talked it over. Finally, Minnie said, “Dear sister, I must find work before our food runs out.” She kissed her mother, wrapped herself up, and left home.

A narrow path led through the forest. Minnie followed it, hoping to reach a place where she might find work. As she hurried on, the shadows grew deeper. Night was coming fast when she saw a very small house, which was a welcome sight. She hurried to it and knocked on the door.

Nobody came to answer her knock. After trying again, she thought that nobody lived there, so she opened the door and walked in, thinking that she would stay for the night. Inside were twelve little beds with their bedclothes all tumbled, twelve little dirty plates on a very dusty table, and a dusty floor.

“Dear me!” said the little girl, “this will never do!” After warming her hands, she worked to make the room clean. She washed the plates, made up the beds, swept the floor, straightened the great rug, and set twelve little chairs in a half circle around the fire.

Just as she finished, the door opened. Twelve of the queerest little people she had ever seen walked in. They were dwarfs. They were surprised and delighted to find their room clean and bright. Minnie explained that she was looking for work because her mother was sick.

The dwarfs thanked Minnie and invited her to eat with them. Their fairy housekeeper was away on holiday, so they asked Minnie to stay and help them. They promised to reward her if she proved faithful and good.

Minnie happily agreed. Each day she cooked breakfast, cleaned the room, mended clothes, and prepared a warm supper. She worked faithfully until the last day of the housekeeper’s holiday.

One day, Minnie looked through a windowpane and saw a beautiful picture of fairy palaces with silver towers and frosted roofs. The sight was so beautiful that she forgot her work until the cuckoo clock struck twelve. She hurried to finish the beds and dishes.

When she took the broom to sweep the floor, she was almost out of time. “I believe,” Minnie said, “that I will not sweep under the rug today. After all, dust cannot be seen under the rug!” She hurried away and left the rug unturned.

That night, a little voice in Minnie’s heart seemed to say, “Dust under the rug! Dust under the rug!” She tried to ignore it, but she could not bear it any longer. She sprang out of bed and swept the dust away.

Surprise! Under the rug were twelve shining gold pieces, as round and bright as the moon. The dwarfs came running to see what had happened.

Minnie told them everything. The dwarfs gathered around her and said that the gold was for her because she had proved faithful and true. They explained that the love that goes with the gold was more valuable than the gold itself. Minnie thanked them and hurried home with her golden treasure, which bought many good things for her mother and little sister.

She never saw the dwarfs again, but she never forgot their lesson to do her work faithfully. From then on, she always swept under the rug.

Honesty is the best policy!
“In the smallest duty done lies wealth of joy forever.”

Teacher Model: How to Find a Story Element

Question: What is the setting of “Dust Under the Rug”?
1

First, I look for clues about where and when the story happens.

2

The story mentions the family home at the edge of a great forest, and later a small house belonging to the dwarfs.

3

It also mentions winter, night, and the changing seasons.

4

Therefore, the setting includes the place and time in which these events occur.

Answer: The story takes place around a home near a great forest and later in the dwarfs’ small house; time clues include winter, night, and the changing seasons.

🧠 THINK DEEPER

Reason With Evidence

Was the gold the most important reward Minnie received?

Think about what the dwarfs said about the gold and the love that went with it. Use evidence from the story.

Sample: The gold was valuable because it helped Minnie’s family, but the lesson about faithfulness and the love connected with the reward was also important. The story shows that Minnie never forgot the lesson.

🔄 REVIEW

Remember This

⭐ Key Ideas

  • Minnie helps her family and searches for work when her mother becomes sick.
  • She cleans the dwarfs’ home and is asked to work faithfully.
  • A hidden task under the rug tests her honesty.
  • The story teaches the value of faithful work and honesty.

📚 Vocabulary

📌 Story Elements

Setting — where and when.

Characters — people, animals, or animated objects.

Plot — sequence of events leading to the climax.

Conclusion — the ending.

⚠️ Common Language Mistake

Incorrect: “Minnie always sweep under the rug.”

Correct: “Minnie always swept under the rug.”

Use the past tense when retelling events that already happened.

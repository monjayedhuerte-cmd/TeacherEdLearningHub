01

Story: Mickey and the Troll

Mickey meets an elderly couple whose young sheep tenders have disappeared. Villagers suspect a troll. Mickey offers to help care for the sheep. When his wooden staff falls into the troll’s well, the troll offers increasingly valuable staffs to test him. Mickey honestly refuses the staffs that are not his. His honesty breaks the spell, frees the young men, and ends the troll’s power.

Main lesson: Honesty matters even when dishonesty could bring a reward.

03

Film lighting

Lighting helps create mood, atmosphere, depth, and texture in a video or film.

  • Key light: the main, strongest light that defines the subject.
  • Fill light: softer light that reduces harsh shadows.
  • Back light: light from behind the subject that separates it from the background.
  • High-key lighting: bright, even lighting with softer shadows; often cheerful or light.
  • Low-key lighting: stronger contrast and deep shadows; often dramatic or mysterious.
  • Daylight: described in the text as producing a blue tone on video.
  • Artificial indoor light: described in the text as creating a reddish-yellow cast.
Recall: key = main, fill = soften, back = separate.
04

Three cases of pronouns

  • Subjective: the pronoun acts as the subject. Examples: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
  • Possessive: shows ownership. Examples: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.
  • Objective: receives an action or follows a preposition. Examples: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.

Subject: They helped. Possessive: The staff is mine. Objective: Mickey helped them.

Quick test: who acts? subject. Whose? possessive. Whom/what receives? objective.

Non-verbal cues

Signals used to communicate without words, such as actions, gestures, and facial expressions.

Memory cue: “No words, still a message.”

Five roles

Repetition repeats; contradiction conflicts; substitution replaces words; complementing adds to words; accenting emphasizes.

Recall: R-C-S-C-A. Explain what each role does.

Facial expressions & gestures

Faces can show feelings. Gestures are deliberate movements such as waving, pointing, or a thumbs-up. Meanings may vary across cultures.

Context matters: a gesture may not mean the same thing everywhere.

Filipino non-verbal cues

Pagmamano shows respect to an elder. Lip pointing may indicate a person or object in the Philippines.

Notice the gesture and the situation.

Pronouns & antecedents

A pronoun refers to a noun. Its antecedent is the word or group of words it refers to. They should agree in number and person, and gender where relevant.

Ask: “Who or what does it refer to?”

Making inferences

An inference is an intelligent guess based on clues in the text and what you already know.

Text clues + what I know = inference.

High and Lifted Up

Tommy is swept along with leaves on a windy day. The introduction reminds readers to see the “big picture” before making decisions.

Think beyond immediate wishes.

Vocabulary & schwa list

Vocabulary: fiercely, porch, swirl, merriment, ponder, fascination, slams, whirlwind. Schwa practice includes mayor, neighbor, serpent, creature, church, burden, perfect, favor, nursery, doctor.

Use each word in a phrase or sentence to remember it.

The 5 Things to Remember

  1. Non-verbal cues: people can communicate without words. Actions can carry messages.
  2. Five roles: repetition, contradiction, substitution, complementing, accenting. Know what each role does.
  3. Pronouns: identify the antecedent and check agreement. Ask who or what it refers to.
  4. Inference: use story clues and what you know. Clues + knowledge.
  5. Story choices: consider the big picture. Think ahead.

30-Second Review

Topic: Non-verbal cues, pronouns, inference, vocabulary.

Key terms: gesture • antecedent • inference • ponder.

Rule: A pronoun should agree with the word it refers to.

Common mistake: Choosing a pronoun without checking its antecedent.

Example: “The houses are simple. They are kept clean.”

03

Cause and Effect

Cause = why something happens. Effect = what happens as a result.

The fields lay unattended, so the earth hardened and cracked.

Signal words: because, so, since, therefore, as a result.
04

Action Verbs

An action verb shows what someone or something does.

The president speaks fluently. Gary painted the wall.

Ask: What action is happening?
05

Linking Verbs

A linking verb joins the subject to a word that describes or identifies it. Examples include is, are, was, were.

The parade was long. The toys are soft.

Linking verbs connect; they do not show an action.
06

Pronouns & Antecedents

A pronoun takes the place of a noun. The antecedent is the word it refers to.

The houses are simple. They are kept clean.

Find the noun first; make the pronoun agree.
07

Making Inferences

An inference is a reasonable conclusion based on clues in the text and what you already know.

Text clues + what I know = inference.

Do not guess randomly. Point to a clue.
08

Non-verbal Cues

Signals communicate without words. Facial expressions and gestures can add meaning to speech.

Five roles: repetition, contradiction, substitution, complementing, accenting.

Notice the action and the situation.

See How It Works

Example 1: Cause → Effect

Clue: Velu's fields lay unattended. The earth hardened and cracked.

Cause: The fields lay unattended.

Effect: The earth hardened and cracked.

Restated: The fields lay unattended, so the earth hardened and cracked.

Example 2: Action or Linking?

“The teachers are inside the library.” — Linking verb; it connects teachers to their location.

“Children depend on their parents.” — Action verb; it expresses what children do.

Example 3: Inference

Velu is worried because his crops need rain and the land is dry.

Inference: Velu is anxious about his family's future.

The conclusion is supported by story clues.

STAGE 4 • APPLY + THINK

Story Lab: Waiting for the Rain

Story snapshot

Velu has worked his land for years. When the rain does not come, the soil becomes dry and barren. He meets an old woman under a tree. She explains that the earth has worked for a long time and, like people, needs rest. Velu begins to understand that allowing the land to rest can help it become ready to grow again when rain returns.

Main lesson: Hard work matters, but wise decisions also consider the bigger picture and the needs of nature.

Summary based on the supplied textbook excerpt; it is not a replacement for reading the complete story.

Think like a reader

Why was Velu dejected?

The rain had not come, the land was dry, and he worried about growing crops and feeding his family.

What did Amma help Velu understand?

The land needs rest after years of cultivation; nature needs time and care.

What is one supported inference about Velu?

He is hardworking and deeply concerned about his family's livelihood. The text describes his long work and his worry about the crops.

What is the cause of the cracked earth in the example?

The fields were left unattended. The hardened, cracked earth is the effect.

Stage 1 · Engage + Connect

Can your devices talk?

Start with a familiar situation.

3–5 minutes

Imagine your classroom

Your teacher sends a document from a laptop to a printer. A classmate opens a shared file on another computer. At home, your phone connects to a Wi-Fi router.

What makes these devices able to exchange information?

Today's mission: By the end, you should be able to classify networks by scope, explain point-to-point networks, and identify protocol, packet, header, payload, router/bridge, and gateway.

Learning objectives

  • Identify scope, architecture, topology, and protocol as ways to classify networks.
  • Distinguish LAN, WAN, MAN, and TAN using their coverage and examples.
  • Explain point-to-point networking and its listed advantages and disadvantages.
  • Describe packets, headers, payloads, routers/bridges, and gateways.

Protocol

A common set of rules and signals that computers use to transfer data. TCP/IP is a widely used Internet protocol suite.

Communication rules

Network scope: small to wide

LAN · Local Area Network

Connects devices within a relatively small area, such as a computer laboratory, home, or building.

TAN · Tiny Area Network

The supplied lesson describes a very small LAN, often around two to four nodes, suitable for a home or small office space.

MAN · Metropolitan Area Network

Connects networks across a city or metropolitan area. Examples in the lesson include offices, a university campus, residential areas, and government buildings.

WAN · Wide Area Network

Connects networks across large geographical areas, potentially spanning cities, states, or countries. The lesson gives the Internet as an example.

Memory cue: LAN = local area; TAN = tiny area in your lesson; MAN = metropolitan/city area; WAN = wide area.

Router/bridge: The supplied lesson says LANs using the same protocols can be connected using a router or bridge. Gateway: A device that links networks using different protocols by translating between them.

Direct point-to-point network

⇄

Only two computers are connected over each communication channel.

Advantages

Flexible, exclusive, and secure. The communication channel is not shared with other pairs.

Disadvantages

Connections and hardware costs can grow quickly as more computers are added. Long-distance connections may be difficult to manage.

Stage 5 · Think & Apply

Design a network for a school

There can be more than one sensible design. Explain your choice.

Challenge brief

A school has a computer laboratory, a library, and an administration office in one campus. A second campus is in another city. Staff need to share selected files between locations.

Which network types could be useful, and why?

Use the terms LAN, WAN, router/bridge, or gateway where relevant.
Sample reasoning: A LAN could connect devices within each building or campus network. A WAN could connect the two campuses across cities. Routers can forward traffic between networks; a gateway is relevant when different protocols need translation. The exact design depends on infrastructure, security, and network requirements.

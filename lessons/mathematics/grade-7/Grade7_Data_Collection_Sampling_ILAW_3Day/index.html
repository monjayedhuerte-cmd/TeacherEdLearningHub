How Do We Collect Data?

I • L • A • W
I INTRODUCE
🕵️

The School Snack Mystery

The school wants to know which snack Grade 7 learners like best. Should the teacher ask everyone, observe choices, or use a questionnaire?

Think: What information do we need, and how can we collect it?

L LEARN
📝

Survey / Questionnaire

People answer prepared questions. It is useful for preferences, opinions, habits, and experiences.

Example: “Which school snack do you prefer?”
👀

Observation

The researcher watches and records what happens without relying only on what people say.

Example: Counting how many learners choose each snack.
🧪

Experiment

The researcher changes a condition and observes the result under planned conditions.

Example: Testing which paper airplane design flies farther.
💬

Interview

The researcher asks questions directly and may ask follow-up questions for deeper answers.

Example: Interviewing learners about study habits.
Remember: A good data collection method should match the question you want to answer.

You want to know whether a huge pot of soup tastes good. Do you need to drink the entire pot? No! A carefully chosen spoonful can tell you a lot.

Final Mission: Build a Fair Survey!

You are now the lead data detective. Your job is to plan a data collection investigation that gives useful and fair information.

L LEARN

What makes a sampling plan strong?

1Clear populationKnow exactly who or what you are studying.
2Appropriate sampleChoose a sample that can represent the population.
3Fair selectionAvoid selecting only people who are easiest to reach.
4Clear questionsAvoid confusing or leading questions.
⚠️ Watch for bias: If your method consistently leaves out certain members, your results may not represent the whole population well.

DAY 1 • 50 MIN

Introduce & Learn: What Is an Inequality?

Goal: distinguish equations from inequalities and understand the four inequality symbols.

I — INTRODUCE • 5 MIN

🚦 The “At Least / At Most” Challenge

Imagine a school activity says: “You must be at least 13 years old.” Does a 13-year-old qualify? What about 14? 12?

AGE ≥ 13

This is not saying there is only one answer. It describes a range of possible answers.

Turn & Talk: Give one real-life rule that uses “more than,” “less than,” “at least,” or “at most.”
L — LEARN • 10 MIN

⚖️ Equation vs. Inequality

Equation

Uses = and says two quantities are exactly equal.

x + 4 = 10

There is one exact value: x = 6.

Inequality

Uses <, >, ≤, or ≥ and compares quantities.

x + 4 > 10

Many values can work: x > 6.

Key idea: an inequality often has many solutions, not just one.

L — LEARN • 10 MIN

🔑 Four Symbols You Must Know

<less thanx < 5 → numbers smaller than 5
>greater thanx > 5 → numbers bigger than 5
≤less than or equal tox ≤ 5 → 5 is included
≥greater than or equal tox ≥ 5 → 5 is included

Memory trick: the wider/open side faces the greater quantity. The symbol points toward the smaller quantity.

DAY 2 • 50 MIN

Learn & Apply: Solving Linear Inequalities

Goal: solve one-variable linear inequalities using inverse operations and preserve the correct inequality direction.

I — INTRODUCE • 5 MIN

🎟️ The Budget Rule

You have ₱100. A snack costs ₱25. You want to know how much money you can spend on another item without going over your budget.

x + 25 ≤ 100

The symbol ≤ matters because spending exactly ₱100 is still allowed.

Question: What operation will help us isolate x?

L — LEARN • 12 MIN

🪜 Solve Like an Equation

Use inverse operations to isolate the variable. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Example 1
x + 7 < 15

Subtract 7 from both sides.

x < 8
Check: choose x = 5 → 5 + 7 = 12 < 15 ✓
Example 2
3x − 4 ≥ 11

Add 4: 3x ≥ 15

Divide by 3: x ≥ 5

L — LEARN • 8 MIN

⚠️ The Special Rule: Negative Multiplication or Division

When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, REVERSE the inequality symbol.
−2x > 10

Divide by −2 and reverse > to <.

x < −5

Why? Multiplying a number line by a negative reverses its direction. This rule protects the truth of the comparison.

A — APPLY • 15 MIN

🎯 Solve It!

1. Solve: x − 6 ≤ 10

2. Solve: 4x > 28

3. Solve: −3x ≤ 18

A — APPLY • 5 MIN

🔍 Catch the Mistake

A learner solves: −5x > 20 → x > −4.

What is wrong?

W — WRAP UP • 5 MIN

⭐ 3-Step Self Check

  1. Isolate the variable using inverse operations.
  2. Reverse the inequality if you multiply/divide by a negative.
  3. Check with a test value.

Explain to a partner: Why does a negative division reverse the symbol?

DAY 3 • 50 MIN

Learn & Apply: Graphing Inequalities on a Number Line

Goal: represent solutions correctly using an open/closed point and shading or arrows.

I — INTRODUCE • 5 MIN

📍 Where Do the Solutions Live?

Suppose the solution is x > 2. Which numbers work? 3? 10? 2? 1?

L — LEARN • 12 MIN

⭕ Open or Closed?

Open Circle ○

Use for < or >. The boundary number is not included.

Example: x > 4

Closed Circle ●

Use for ≤ or ≥. The boundary number is included.

Example: x ≥ 4

L — LEARN • 8 MIN

➡️ Which Direction?

−2−101234
x > 1 → open at 1, shade RIGHT
−2−101234
x ≤ 1 → closed at 1, shade LEFT

Shortcut: “greater” points right; “less” points left.

A — APPLY • 15 MIN

A — APPLY • 5 MIN

🧪 Test a Point

  • Choose open for < or >, closed for ≤ or ≥.

DAY 4 • 50 MIN

Apply: Multi-Step Inequalities

Goal: solve inequalities with variables on one or both sides and communicate the solution clearly.

I — INTRODUCE • 5 MIN

🏁 Two Paths, One Finish

Sometimes an inequality has x on both sides. The goal is still the same: get x alone.

5x − 3 > 2x + 12

We can subtract 2x from both sides first.

L — LEARN • 12 MIN

🪜 Worked Example: Variable on Both Sides

5x − 3 > 2x + 12

Subtract 2x: 3x − 3 > 12

Add 3: 3x > 15

Divide by 3: x > 5

Notice: we divided by a positive number, so the symbol stayed the same.

L — LEARN • 8 MIN

🧩 Another Example

2(x − 3) ≤ 10

Distribute: 2x − 6 ≤ 10

Add 6: 2x ≤ 16

Divide by 2: x ≤ 8

Important: distributing is a useful first step when parentheses appear.

DAY 5 • 50 MIN

Apply & Wrap Up: Real-Life Inequality Problems

Goal: translate situations into inequalities, solve them, graph solutions, and interpret the answer.

I — INTRODUCE • 5 MIN

🌟 Inequalities in Real Life

Inequalities appear when there is a limit, minimum, maximum, capacity, requirement, or range.

At least

minimum

x ≥ 10

At most

maximum

x ≤ 10

Words give clues, but always ask: Is the boundary included?

L — LEARN • 10 MIN

🧠 Word-Problem Method

  1. Define the unknown.
  2. Translate the words into an inequality.
  3. Solve using inverse operations.
  4. Graph the solution.
  5. Interpret the answer in context.
Example: A student needs at least 80 points. She already has 56. How many more points x are needed?

56 + x ≥ 80

x ≥ 24

Interpretation: She needs at least 24 more points.

L — LEARN • 10 MIN

📐 Geometry Example

A rectangle has width 6 cm and length x cm. Its perimeter is at most 40 cm.

Perimeter: 2x + 2(6) ≤ 40

2x + 12 ≤ 40

2x ≤ 28

x ≤ 14

Because a length cannot be negative, the meaningful dimensions satisfy 0 < x ≤ 14.

A — APPLY • 10 MIN

💰 Money Mission

You have ₱500. You plan to buy a notebook for ₱85 and several identical pens costing ₱35 each. What is the greatest number of pens you can buy?

Model: 85 + 35x ≤ 500 → 35x ≤ 415 → x ≤ 11.857... Since pens are whole items, the greatest possible number is 11.

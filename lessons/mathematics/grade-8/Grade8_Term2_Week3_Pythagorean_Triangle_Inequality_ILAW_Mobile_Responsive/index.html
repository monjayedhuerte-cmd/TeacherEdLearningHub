I — INTRODUCEL — LEARN

🎯 Mission: Find the Missing Distance

A ladder reaches a wall. The bottom is 6 m from the wall and the ladder is 10 m long. How high does it reach?

1. What makes a right triangle?

A right triangle has one angle measuring exactly 90°. The two sides forming the right angle are the legs. The side opposite 90° is the hypotenuse.

legleghypotenuse90°
Remember:

The hypotenuse is always the longest side of a right triangle and is always opposite the right angle.

2. Discover the relationship

For a right triangle with legs a and b, and hypotenuse c, the areas built on the sides reveal:

a² + b² = c²

This is the Pythagorean Theorem.

Example: If a = 6 and b = 8, then 6² + 8² = c² → 36 + 64 = 100 → c² = 100 → c = 10.

⚡ Quick Check

Which side must be opposite the 90° angle?

DAY 2
Learn & Apply50 minutes • Finding Missing Sides

📐 The 4-Step Method

1Identify the hypotenuse.
2Write a² + b² = c².
3Substitute known lengths.
4Solve and check.

Worked Example A — Missing Hypotenuse

Legs are 9 cm and 12 cm.

c² = 9² + 12²c² = 81 + 144 = 225c = √225 = 15 cm

Worked Example B — Missing Leg

Hypotenuse is 13 m and one leg is 5 m. Find the other leg, x.

x² + 5² = 13²x² = 169 − 25 = 144x = √144 = 12 m
Why subtraction? When the missing value is a leg, rearrange: x² = c² − known leg².

🔄 The Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem

Normally, we start with a right triangle and find a side. The converse works backward: given three side lengths, we can determine the type of triangle.

For sides a ≤ b ≤ c:
If a² + b² = c² → RIGHT
If a² + b² > c² → ACUTE
If a² + b² < c² → OBTUSE

Example 1

3, 4, 5

3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²

RIGHT TRIANGLE

Example 2

5, 5, 6

5² + 5² = 50 > 36 = 6²

ACUTE TRIANGLE

🚀 Exit Ticket

Can 2, 3, 7 form a triangle? Use triangle inequality from tomorrow's lesson to explain why.

No. The two shorter sides must add to more than the longest side: 2 + 3 = 5, and 5 is not greater than 7.

DAY 4
Learn & Apply50 minutes • Triangle Inequality Theorem

📏 Can These Three Lengths Make a Triangle?

Not every three positive lengths can form a triangle. The key rule is:

a + b > c

when c is the longest side. In fact, each pair of sides must have a sum greater than the third side.

Example: 5, 7, 10

Check the largest side: 5 + 7 = 12 > 10. Also, 5 + 10 > 7 and 7 + 10 > 5. Therefore, yes, a triangle is possible.

Example: 3, 4, 8

Check: 3 + 4 = 7, and 7 is not greater than 8. Therefore, no triangle can be formed.

🔍 Find the Possible Range

Two sides are 8 cm and 11 cm. What values could the third side x have?

|11 − 8| < x < 11 + 8 → 3 < x < 19

💡 Connection

Triangle inequality is useful in construction, map reading, design, surveying, and checking whether measured lengths are physically possible.

Three Questions About Two Lines

5 min hook • 10 min concept • 15 min examples • 15 min interaction • 5 min exit

🚦 Hook: What Can Two Lines Do?

Imagine two roads drawn on the same map. They may meet once, never meet, or lie on top of each other. Linear systems behave similarly.

📘 Learn

A system of two linear equations can be classified by the number of solutions. When represented as lines, focus on their relationship.

One solution
Lines intersect once.
1 common ordered pair.
No solution
Distinct parallel lines.
0 common ordered pairs.
Infinitely many
Same line.
Infinitely many common ordered pairs.

🧠 Say It

Complete: “The solution of a system is a point that __________.”

Expected idea: satisfies both equations.

WEEK 2 • DAY 2 • ILAW: LEARN • 50 MIN

One Solution: Lines Meet Once

5 min review • 10 min model • 15 min guided graph reading • 15 min practice • 5 min wrap-up

📈 The Picture

Consider y = x + 1 and y = −x + 5. Their slopes are different, so the lines meet at one point.

y = x + 1    |    y = −x + 5

Set the y-values equal: x + 1 = −x + 5 → 2x = 4 → x = 2. Then y = 3.

✅ One solution: (2, 3)

🎯 Check

If two lines intersect at exactly one point, how many solutions does the system have?

🔎 Verify

Does (2,3) satisfy both equations above?

WEEK 2 • DAY 3 • ILAW: LEARN + APPLY • 50 MIN

No Solution: Parallel Lines

5 min warm-up • 10 min concept • 15 min examples • 15 min interactive sorting • 5 min exit

🚫 What Does “No Solution” Mean?

Two distinct parallel lines never meet. Therefore there is no ordered pair that makes both equations true.

y = 2x + 1    |    y = 2x − 4

Both have slope 2, but different y-intercepts. Same slope + different intercepts → parallel distinct lines.

❌ Number of solutions: 0

🎮 Sort It

System: y = 3x + 2 and y = 3x + 2. Is it no solution?

💡 Key Distinction

Same slope + different intercept
Parallel → no solution.
Same equation
Same line → infinitely many.
Different slopes
Meet once → one solution.

WEEK 3 • DAY 1 • ILAW: INTRODUCE + LEARN • 50 MIN

Why Solve Algebraically?

5 min hook • 10 min concept • 15 min model • 15 min guided practice • 5 min exit

🔍 From Graph to Algebra

Graphing gives a visual answer. Algebraic methods let us find the exact solution using equations and operations.

Example: x + y = 7 and x − y = 1. If we add the equations, y disappears:

2x = 8 → x = 4 → y = 3
Solution: (4,3)

🧠 The Strategy

  1. Choose a variable to eliminate or isolate.
  2. Perform the same valid operation to both sides.
  3. Solve for one variable.
  4. Substitute to find the other.
  5. Check both equations.
WEEK 3 • DAY 2 • ILAW: LEARN • 50 MIN

Substitution Method

5 min review • 10 min method • 15 min worked example • 15 min practice • 5 min wrap-up

🪜 Step-by-Step

Solve: y = x + 2 and x + y = 8.

  1. Substitute y = x + 2 into x + y = 8.
  2. x + (x + 2) = 8
  3. 2x + 2 = 8 → 2x = 6 → x = 3
  4. y = 3 + 2 = 5
✅ (3,5)

🎯 Your Turn

Using y = x + 1 and x + y = 7, what is x?

💡 Why Substitute?

Substitution replaces one variable with an equivalent expression, allowing the system to become an equation with one variable.

WEEK 3 • DAY 3 • ILAW: LEARN + APPLY • 50 MIN

Elimination by Addition

5 min warm-up • 10 min concept • 15 min model • 15 min practice • 5 min exit

➕ Make a Variable Disappear

Solve: x + y = 10 and x − y = 2.

Add the equations:

x+y + x−y = 10+2 → 2x = 12 → x = 6

Then substitute x = 6 into x + y = 10 → y = 4.

Solution: (6,4)

🎮 Practice

What disappears when you add the equations above?

⚠️ Accuracy Tip

Line up like terms. Add constants carefully. After finding one variable, always substitute to find the other.

Elimination with Multiplication

5 min review • 10 min setup • 15 min teacher model • 15 min learner practice • 5 min check

🔧 When Coefficients Do Not Match

Consider 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2. The y terms already have opposite coefficients, so addition works immediately:

3x = 9 → x = 3 → y = 1
Solution: (3,1)

Systems in Real-Life Situations

5 min hook • 10 min modeling • 15 min translating • 15 min practice • 5 min exit

🛍️ Hook: Two Kinds of Tickets

A school event sells adult tickets for ₱60 and student tickets for ₱40. A total of 10 tickets earns ₱480.

Let x = adult tickets, y = student tickets.

x + y = 10
60x + 40y = 480

The first equation represents the number of tickets; the second represents the total money.

🎯 Identify the Meaning

What does x represent?

WEEK 4 • DAY 3 • ILAW: APPLY • 50 MIN

Solve and Interpret a Word Problem

5 min warm-up • 10 min plan • 15 min algebra • 15 min interpretation • 5 min exit

🍎 Example

A fruit stand sells apples at ₱20 each and oranges at ₱15 each. A customer buys 8 fruits for ₱140. How many apples and oranges?

Let x = apples, y = oranges.

x+y=8
20x+15y=140

Multiply the first equation by 15: 15x+15y=120. Subtract from the second: 5x=20, so x=4. Then y=4.

✅ 4 apples and 4 oranges.

🎯 Interpretation Check

Why must we reject an algebraic answer if it gives a negative number of tickets?

WEEK 4 • DAY 4 • ILAW: APPLY + WRAP-UP • 50 MIN

Money, Number, and Geometry Problems

5 min review • 10 min problem types • 20 min guided solving • 10 min learner challenge • 5 min reflection

💰 Money Problems

Define variables, write a quantity equation and a value equation, solve, then state the answer with units.

🔢 Number Problems

Translate relationships such as sum/difference into equations.

📐 Geometry Problems

Use formulas or relationships to express two facts with two variables.

🧠 Problem-Solving Routine

  1. Understand: What is known and unknown?
  2. Define: What do x and y mean?
  3. Model: Write two equations.
  4. Solve: Use substitution or elimination.
  5. Interpret: Answer the actual question.
  6. Check: Does the answer make sense?

🎯 Final Practice

If x+y=12 and x−y=4, which ordered pair solves the system?

Irrelevance of "Life and Works of Rizal" to Music Students

A student perspective on curriculum relevance, specialized training, and the place of general education in a Bachelor of Music program.

Music Performance · Piano Emphasis Universidad de Sta. Isabel Opinion Article
This article questions the course’s fit within a music performance curriculum—not the historical importance of José Rizal or the value of Philippine history.

01 A Question of Relevance

As a college student pursuing a Bachelor of Music in Music Performance (Piano Emphasis) at Universidad de Sta. Isabel, I find it unnecessary for “Life and Works of Rizal” to be a required course in my program. The course may teach us about Rizal’s contributions to Philippine history, but its necessity for my field can be questioned in light of the specific skills, practice hours, rehearsals, and demanding instrument mastery that music performance requires.

Music students spend years developing their skills through practice, theory and harmony, composition, music history, and other areas directly related to their future careers as performers. In comparison, studying Rizal’s biography, novels, and historical contributions does not necessarily improve our musical technique or instrumental proficiency. With demanding practice schedules and academic requirements, a course with little direct application to our specialization can feel like an additional burden.

02 The Opportunity Cost

Another concern is the opportunity cost of requiring the subject. College students have limited time and energy, and every required course takes time away from other academic activities. Instead of devoting that time to practice and rehearsals, music students must also complete readings, written assignments, and projects related to “Life and Works of Rizal.” Although these tasks may develop general academic skills, they can contribute to an overloaded curriculum that does not always prioritize the practical needs of music students.

03 Cultural Learning Through Music

The goal of teaching national identity and appreciation for Philippine culture can also be supported through “Music of the Philippines 1 & 2,” major subjects that music students already take and that are more directly connected to our discipline. Filipino folk songs, Indigenous musical instruments and traditions, kundiman, and the works of Filipino composers provide meaningful opportunities to understand and appreciate the country’s cultural heritage through music itself.

Central distinction

Questioning whether a course is required in its current form is not the same as dismissing the historical importance of José Rizal.

04 Preserve the Legacy, Rethink the Approach

Arguing that “Life and Works of Rizal” is unnecessary for Bachelor of Music students does not mean that Rizal himself is irrelevant or that his contributions should be disregarded. His writings and contributions remain essential to understanding Philippine history. The more reasonable criticism is that requiring every college student to study the course in exactly the same manner may not be the most effective approach for all academic disciplines.

The content and teaching methods could be adapted to different programs so that students can better understand the course’s relevance to their future professions. Such an approach would preserve the importance of Rizal’s legacy while making the learning experience more meaningful across disciplines.

05 Conclusion

“Life and Works of Rizal” may be considered unnecessary in its current form for music students because it has little direct relation to specialized training and adds to an already demanding workload. Although learning about Rizal has historical value, universities should also consider whether the course requirements justify the time taken away from practice, rehearsals, and other activities essential to musical development.

A more flexible approach could preserve the importance of Rizal’s legacy while allowing music students to focus more effectively on musical growth and performance preparation. Rather than dismissing Philippine history, the goal should be to ensure that general education courses contribute meaningfully to every college student’s education. - Verdejo K.
